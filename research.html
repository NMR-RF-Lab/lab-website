Facilities

On campus · Emerging Technologies Building

NMR RF Lab

The NMR RF Lab primarily operates in a 500 sq ft lab space in the Emerging Technologies Building on the main campus of Texas A&M University.

  • State-of-the-art Agilent network analyzers
  • Reflow soldering system
  • Printed circuit board prototyping capabilities
  • Small instrumentation for RF coil fabrication
  • 3D printing capabilities
  • Computing support for Remcom XFdtd®
Off campus · University Services Building

Magnetic Resonance Systems Lab

Scanner access is located off campus in the MRSL, in 9000 sq ft of research space in the University Services Building.

  • 4.7T/33cm MRI scanner, supported by Varian Unity/Inova spectrometer
  • 4.7T/40cm scanner with RF screen room, supported by Varian Unity/Inova spectrometer
  • 1T/20cm Samsung extremity scanner
  • Light machine shop
  • Small animal handling area, anesthesia, gating, and monitoring equipment

Advanced Imaging Research Center

High field imaging is performed off-site in collaboration with the Advanced Imaging Research Center at UT Southwestern Medical Center in Dallas, which houses a Philips 7.0T Achieva System.

Example projects

What we are building

Hardware & software development for accessible NMR-based biomarker detection of diseases

The full potential of NMR to access chemical information beyond 1H imaging is scarcely realized, largely due to the lack of sensitivity to nuclei other than hydrogen. In particular, more accessible low field and clinical field strengths are generally not used for non-1H imaging or spectroscopy, although a wealth of information regarding disease state is present in other nuclei such as 31P and 23Na.

We develop hardware-based approaches to increase the sensitivity in detection of these potentially powerful biomarkers of disease and software approaches to ease processing challenges.

Hardware development for high field imaging and spectroscopy

The NMR RF Lab has worked in close collaboration with the Advanced Imaging Research Center at UT Southwestern Medical Center in Dallas, which houses one of two 7T scanners in the state of Texas. RF design at high fields is challenging due to the interactions of the associated higher frequencies with the human body.

We develop novel hardware approaches toward harnessing the potential of high field MR imaging and spectroscopy to help us characterize and understand disease.

Optimization of small animal imaging

Animal imaging is a known challenge due to the smaller size and faster heart rate of the subject. There has been an ongoing effort in the lab to optimize this process overall and per-application-at-hand through the use of new coil designs and parallel imaging.

Approaches to RF front end design to enable flexibility and portability

Multiple-channel receivers are not commonly available for non-hydrogen frequencies. Our interest in the intersection of parallel imaging and non-proton spectroscopy drives work in new approaches to receiver design.
